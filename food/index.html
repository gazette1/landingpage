Nobody publishes how much of the funded benefit goes unused. The two research briefings behind this page (October 4 and October 23, 2026) found the public data thin: MedPAC could not treat reported meal encounters as reliable, and a Commonwealth Fund survey found fewer than one in ten Medicare Advantage respondents reporting use of food or meal benefits. That number is the first thing the plan calls will ask for.

02 / The insight, and Richard had it first

The bottleneck is utilization, not supply.

Plans and programs fund food benefits that eligible people never claim. Trusted local people get those benefits used better than a plan call center does. The research found the thesis sound. What failed was the business model around it.

03 / What the first version taught

Good idea, venture-shaped business

SecureFOOD, 2023 to 2026
  • Raised venture-style money and sold software to large national insurers.
  • Insurers take 9 to 18 months to buy and already had food vendors.
  • Federal support for food benefits narrowed in 2025 and 2026.
  • Changed direction three times. No public record of a paying customer.
This version
  • A nonprofit. Grants and community-benefit money a company cannot take.
  • Local. Maryland and Delaware, a few dozen partners. Never national.
  • A service with people in it. Not a platform.
  • Funded by whoever loses money when a benefit goes unused.
04 / What we do

We coordinate and arrange. We never deliver food.

  1. 01
    ReferralA hospital, plan, or food bank refers a patient.
  2. 02
    EnrollA navigator calls, confirms eligibility, and finds the benefits they already have.
  3. 03
    ArrangeWe order meals or groceries from a provider that already delivers.
  4. 04
    Check inText and phone check-ins. Problems go to the care team.
  5. 05
    ReportEvery quarter, each funder sees who used what and what changed.
The product
The report is the product.

Each funder sees what it paid for inside one budget year, which the first version never gave anyone. Two navigators, AI for reminders and reports, off-the-shelf HIPAA tools. No platform.

Five things we will not build
  • No kitchen, truck, or inventory. MANNA, Moveable Feast, and the food banks deliver. We are their partner, not their rival.
  • No app or platform. Off-the-shelf tools and secure file exchange. People do the work.
  • No national expansion. Two states. More only after the first two work.
  • No venture money. Contracts and grants. A board, not investors.
  • No bet on new reimbursement. Nothing here waits on a waiver or a new billing code.
05 / Where

Maryland and Delaware first, on purpose

FirstMaryland
Why

Richard did the groundwork: Baltimore, CareFirst, the Techstars and UpSurge network.

The money logic

Hospitals run on fixed state-set budgets under the AHEAD model, so an avoided readmission saves them the avoidable cost this year. In April 2026 the state launched a medically tailored meals program with Moveable Feast and Food & Friends that covers Prince George's County. The enrollment gap in that program is the first thing to ask about.

FirstDelaware
Why

My own network: hospital, state agency, plan, and nonprofit contacts I already know.

The money logic

No fixed budget, so the buyer is a plan, a community-benefit office, or the state. ChristianaCare, Beebe, and Bayhealth already fund food access; the state issued a food-is-medicine RFP in 2026. Each is a separate call.

LaterThe corridor
Only if the first two work

Prince George's County beyond the first cohort, DC, Philadelphia, Northern Virginia.

Why it can wait

Medicare Advantage plans are federal, so a plan contract can reach across the corridor before we do.

07 / Follow the money for one person

We are paid for the work, never for the food

A 74-year-old on a Medicare Advantage plan goes home after a heart failure stay. Her plan already pays for a food benefit she has never used. Every return visit costs the hospital.

Who pays usWhat they are paying forWhat we get
The hospitalFewer readmissions. It refers 75 patients and funds a six-month program.$40,000 per program, about $530 a patient
The health planNavigation work for members it assigns to us, with agreed completion measures.A funded assignment. No price yet.
The meal provider or food bankA grant that says enroll 500 people, and no staff to find them.An enrollment subcontract, about $40,000 a year

The meals are paid for by the plan's benefit, the hospital, or the provider's grant. We are paid for finding people, enrolling them, keeping them on it, and proving it worked. The quarterly report is why each funder renews.

Every amount is an assumption. None of the four has agreed to pay yet; the first 50 calls find out whether they will, and at what price.

08 / The front door

The members already gather on Sunday

Large congregations in Prince George's County

Thousands of members on Medicare and Medicaid, a health ministry, a room, and volunteers who are already trusted.

Maryland has done this before: the Faith Community Health Network (LifeBridge Health, 2016) linked hospital navigators to trained volunteers in 70 congregations, with a signed consent, and reported lower inpatient use.

  • A referral source: members with a food benefit they never use.
  • A site: an enrollment Sunday with a navigator at a table.
  • A volunteer pipeline: two members trained as navigators.
  • Proof of reach when we sell a plan contract.
What it cannot be
A payer.

A church does not buy programs. A plan or hospital still funds each member we enroll. So the congregation makes the plan contract easier to sell. It does not replace it.
