How it works

      

        
The workflow. An analyst sets assumptions for one property. Code drafts every budget line from the trailing twelve months, finds the outliers, and tests debt service coverage the way the lender will. An AI model drafts a variance note for each flag. A gate rejects any note with a dollar amount, percentage, or coverage ratio that is not on the budget. A person decides each flag, and the budget moves to approval with an audit trail tied to the exact assumptions.

        
What the model is allowed to do. Write. It never computes a number. The notes were drafted once for each property's starting assumptions by  and checked by the gate; when you change an assumption, the page switches to rule-based notes because the AI draft no longer matches the numbers.

        
What the flags look for. An expense line that moves more than 10 percent from the trailing year (high above 20), a line more than 25 percent off the peer median per unit, vacancy, concessions, or bad debt budgeted more than 1 point better than last year's actual, coverage below or near the covenant, and coverage that only clears the covenant because of those optimistic assumptions.

        
Not real data. The three properties, their histories, the peer medians, and the loans are synthetic, built to look like typical DC-area assets. Not affiliated with any operator, lender, or software company.
