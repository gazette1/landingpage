    What is his, what is mine

    His published framework JP
    • Four decision areas: operational or strategic; value, technical and execution feasibility; technology sourcing; approach to ROI.
    • The three gates in order: "should we do it", "can it be done", "can we do it".
    • Start from the ideal inputs, then build a feature inventory (sources, formats, history, geography, interval) and identify what is unavailable or needs engineering.
    • Computational suitability and the accuracy threshold set by the actual use; prototype to production as a separate test.
    • Two sensible resource paths: fit the project to fixed resources, or fund the requirements of a desired outcome.
    • Three sources of capability: industry-agnostic applications, internal development, third-party integration components.
    • Conventional ROI for predictable automation; decompose and evaluate holistically for strategic work; evaluate uncertainty rather than hide it.
    My additions RH
    • The five cases, the data inventories and every verdict. Four cases come from work I do as a CRE credit analyst and LIHTC advisor.
    • A written decision rule that turns gate results into Go, Modify or Don't go, with later gates locked when an earlier one fails. He publishes no scoring system, so this is mine and it is deliberately not a weighted score.
    • The decision record: the smallest next step, a kill rule, and what would change the decision.
    • The uncertainty register inside the returns area.
    • The verification gate pattern (every number in an AI draft must match a computed fact) used in cases 02 and 05.
