The plain reading

Both of you spend what comes in. Russ ends most months where he started. Jael's personal accounts are flat too, but only because the LLC pays about $4,000 a month of household and personal bills, and its balance fell $10,318 in three months while revenue dropped from $11,341 in June to $4,131 in August.

With the Mary and Main paychecks gone, June to August habits cannot continue. The household has to take about $3,300 a month out of spending to break even, and more to save. The habit table and the Q4 budget below do exactly that.

What is already working

The rent has been shared, not carried by one person. Russ pays $1,690 of shared costs directly and the LLC pays $2,182 on Jael's behalf: 44 percent to 56 percent. With the new incomes ($6,478 against $5,000) Russ now earns 56 percent, so a fair split moves the other way: Russ carries more of the joint account, Jael less. The bigger problems are that nothing is being saved and that the business is carrying personal costs.

§ 10 / Joint account rules

Seven rules.

Short enough to remember. The budget only works if the plumbing changes with it.

  1. 01Open one joint checking account and put every shared bill on autopay from it: rent to Lisa-Marie Thompson, BGE, Verizon, T-Mobile, Vivint, renters insurance, cleaning. Groceries and shared meals go on one joint debit card.
  2. 02Each person sends their share the day the money lands, as a standing transfer, not a manual one. Russ: half his share on each PBS payday. Jael: her share the day the $3,000 arrives, topped up from the business transfer on the 1st.
  3. 03The LLC pays only LLC costs plus one fixed $2,000 transfer to Jael on the 1st. No rent, no Honda, no BGE, no Amazon from the Wells Fargo account. The $3,000 from Mary and Main lands in Jael's personal checking, not the LLC.
  4. 04The Amex becomes a personal card with a hard rule: no business charges. If the LLC needs a card, it gets its own. Pay the Amex in full from the joint travel line and Jael's personal line until it is at zero, then keep it there.
  5. 05The home fund is a separate high-yield savings account in both names, funded only from the joint account, never swept back. Russ's Performance Savings has been used as a holding tank; this one is not.
  6. 06Fifteen minutes every Sunday. Open the joint account, check the two personal ceilings, and note anything that will break next week. No blame in the meeting; the numbers do the talking.
  7. 07Explain the three LLC items (the Aug 14 cash, the $3,300 payroll destination, the Comptroller payment) before the January review, and pull the Capital One and Navy Federal balances into the next version.
